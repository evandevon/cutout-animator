Sound check

Sounds cutting out or silent? On Windows, an open microphone can make the computer act like it's on a phone call and mute other sounds. Fix it once in Windows: Sound settings › More sound settings › Communications › "Do nothing". The level meter pauses by itself while you play a sound. If sounds still cut out, untick "Show the microphone level all the time". Leave clean-up off unless recordings have the music in them twice.

How it works

Cutout Animator lives at evandevon.github.io/cutout-animator. Open it in a browser on any computer, tablet or phone.

  1. Add a drawing. Photograph it on white paper and the paper is cut away. Draw around it or use the brushes to tidy it up.
  2. Or use the library. In the Cutouts tab, choose ready-made people, animals, buildings, vehicles and more.
  3. Set the scene. Choose a backdrop, background sound and music, then place your cutouts. Tap a cutout and pick an animation at the top of the stage: sway, hop, float or spin. Tap it again to stop. Park cutouts in the dark "off stage" areas at the sides so they can walk on later. Those areas aren't in the video.
  4. Press Record. Tell the story and drag your cutouts at the same time. Several people can drag different cutouts on a touch screen.
  5. Teach a move. Tap a cutout, press Teach a move, then drag it and let go. It repeats that move for the whole recording. If you grab it, it follows you, then glides back to where you grabbed it and carries on.
  6. Sound effects. In the Sounds tab, record your own sounds first. Tick the ones you want on each scene and they become big buttons under the stage. Tap them while recording, or press their number keys, so one person can do sounds while another moves the cutouts. Teachers can turn on a ready-made sound library in the ☰ menu.
  7. More scenes. ＋ Blank makes a new empty scene. Continue story makes a new scene that starts where the selected one finishes, with the same cutouts, backdrop and sounds. Hold and drag a scene to move it. Tap 🗑 on the selected scene to delete it.
  8. Add more. Plays the scene back so you can move another cutout or add more voice. This is how to move several cutouts with a mouse.
  9. Make video makes one video of the whole story, or a separate video for each scene. Save project keeps a file you can open later and keep editing.

Templates for a whole class

In the ☰ menu, print a template. Students draw a backdrop and characters, write a script, tick a background sound and music, and note the sounds they'll make. Then choose Add filled-in templates and pick the scanned JPGs or PDFs, or use a webcam. Each sheet becomes one scene, and the script shows on screen during recording.

Pinned cutouts

Tap a cutout and press 📌 Pinned for things that are part of the place, like trees and houses. A pinned cutout stays put while recording, and taps go straight through it to the cutouts behind. You can still move it while setting up. Library buildings, trees and rocks start pinned. Tap 📌 again to unpin.

Sticking cutouts together

Tap a cutout, press 🔗 Stick to…, then tap what it should stick to, like a hat onto a head or a rider onto a horse. The main one gets a yellow 🔗 circle in its middle: drag that circle to move it with everything stuck to it. Dragging anywhere else moves just that one cutout. If the main one turns, grows or hops, the stuck ones go with it. While recording, pinned cutouts can't be grabbed at all, but a pinned cutout still goes along when the cutout it's stuck to is moved by its 🔗 circle. Press 🔗 again to unstick.

Moving cutouts

Drag to move, with a finger or the mouse. Pinch with two fingers, or use the round handle or mouse wheel, to resize and turn. Hold Shift with the wheel to turn. Double-tap to flip.

Keyboard

Tab chooses a cutout. Arrows move it, [ ] resize, , . turn, F flips, R records, Esc stops.
